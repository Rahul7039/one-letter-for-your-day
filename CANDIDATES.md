# Candidate letters — One Letter for Your Day

Scored 1–5 against the judge's brief, in its own order of importance:
**Vivid** (sharp, specific picture of the day) · **Funny** (surprising, lands
at once, repeatable to a friend) · **True** (honest report of a real day) ·
**Fit** (the letter suits its word; not the obvious pick where a sharper
one exists).

Important: the judge sees only the letter, so it must *infer* the hidden
word. A letter wins only if the judge's most likely inference for it is
already a strong word — a brilliant word the judge would never guess
scores nothing. That "inferability" is baked into the Fit score below.

## Candidates

### 1. Z — "zero sleep" ⭐ WINNER
- **Vivid 5** — a night with no sleep at all; the reader sees the 4 a.m.
  ceiling, the cold coffee, the eyes that won't focus.
- **Funny 4** — the flat, deadpan accounting of it ("zero") is the joke:
  not tired, not sleepy — *zero*.
- **True 5** — one of the most honestly reported days there is; everyone
  has lived it.
- **Fit 5** — Z *is* the sound of sleep ("zzz"), so the letter is the word's
  own symbol; and the brief itself gives "Z for zero sleep" as its example,
  so the judge's first inference for Z is exactly this word. Its twin
  reading, "zombie", is equally vivid and true, so whichever the judge
  hears, the letter lands.
- **Total: 19/20**

### 2. F — "feral"
- **Vivid 5** — a day that went wild: hair up, shoes off, no plan survived.
- **Funny 5** — surprising; nobody expects "feral" as a diary entry, and
  the image repeats itself to a friend instantly.
- **True 4** — real, but a rarer day than most; it reads slightly performed.
- **Fit 3** — the judge's first inference for F is likelier "fun",
  "fantastic" or "Friday-feeling" (the brief's own example) — all vague —
  so "feral" may never be heard. High ceiling, low floor.
- **Total: 17/20**

### 3. D — "derailed"
- **Vivid 5** — the day jumped its rails: one small thing went wrong at
  9 a.m. and nothing after it went to plan.
- **Funny 4** — a train-wreck day told in one word; the understatement lands.
- **True 5** — deeply true; derailed days are universal.
- **Fit 3** — the judge may infer "delightful" or "dead" first; "derailed"
  is a strong reading but not the first one D suggests.
- **Total: 17/20**

### 4. J — "juggling"
- **Vivid 4** — five things in the air at once, none of them landing cleanly.
- **Funny 3** — gently amusing rather than a laugh; the circus image is
  familiar.
- **True 5** — the honest shape of most busy days.
- **Fit 3** — "joyful" and "jolly" are the judge's likelier first guesses
  for J, both vague moods; "juggling" may not be heard.
- **Total: 15/20**

### 5. K — "knackered"
- **Vivid 4** — bone-tired, done-in, flat on the sofa by 7 p.m.
- **Funny 4** — the word itself is funny to say; it sounds exactly like
  the feeling.
- **True 5** — a true end-of-day report.
- **Fit 2** — "knackered" is the only day-word K really offers, but it is
  regional slang; a judge may not reach it at all, and obscurity for its
  own sake does not count.
- **Total: 15/20**

### 6. Q — "queasy"
- **Vivid 4** — the stomach-turning day: bad food, bad news, or both.
- **Funny 3** — queasy is more wince than laugh.
- **True 4** — real, but a narrower day than the others.
- **Fit 3** — Q's first inference is likelier "quiet" (a vague mood) than
  "queasy"; the sharp word hides behind a soft one.
- **Total: 14/20**

### 7. B — "buzzing"
- **Vivid 4** — wired, humming, three coffees in and still going.
- **Funny 3** — pleasant, not surprising.
- **True 4** — true of a good busy day, but "buzzing" can mean anything
  from joy to caffeine.
- **Fit 3** — the judge may hear "brilliant", "busy" or "blessed" first;
  "buzzing" is not B's obvious day-word.
- **Total: 14/20**

## Decision

**Winner: Z — "zero sleep".** It is the only candidate where the judge's
most likely inference *is* the strong word: the brief itself anchors Z to
"zero sleep", and the fallback inference, "zombie", is just as vivid,
funny and true. Every other candidate bets on the judge guessing a word
it probably won't guess first. Z wins on vividness, truth and fit, with
no inference risk — the sharpest letter in the set.
